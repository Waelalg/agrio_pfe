\chapter{Detailed Paper-by-Paper Literature Matrix}

This appendix records how the literature was filtered for the first chapters and comparative analysis. Review, overview, systematic review, bibliometric review, and survey papers were not used as main technical evidence, figure sources, or definition sources. They were consulted only to understand terminology and to identify original research papers.

\section{Excluded review and orientation sources}

The following papers were excluded from the main source pool because they are review, overview, bibliometric, systematic, or context papers rather than original prototype, case-study, or experimental contributions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Subeesh and Chauhan, agricultural Digital Twin review.
    \item Zhang et al., comprehensive review of Digital Twin technology in agriculture.
    \item Gund et al., bibliometric review of Digital Twin applications in smart agriculture.
    \item Awais et al., review of precision agriculture through Digital Twins and smart farming technologies.
    \item Tsaousidis et al., AI-enabled Digital Twins in agriculture.
    \item Ahsen et al., systematic review on Digital Twins for agricultural water management.
    \item Garcia et al., overview of IoT-based smart irrigation systems.
    \item Obaideen et al., overview of smart irrigation systems using IoT.
    \item Del-Coco et al., review of machine learning for smart irrigation.
    \item Morchid et al., review of IoT and machine-learning applications in sustainable irrigation management.
\end{itemize}

These sources may help explain the broader research context, but the thesis avoids using them as primary evidence for technical definitions, implementation figures, and the comparative table.

\section{Original research papers retained as main source pool}

\subsection{Kodali and Sarjerao, 2017}

Focus: Low-cost MQTT smart irrigation.

Main contribution: The work presents a practical irrigation prototype using soil-moisture sensing, ESP8266/NodeMCU communication, MQTT messaging, pump control, and web/mobile visualization.

Use in this thesis: This paper supports the MQTT and low-cost actuator-control discussion because it is an original prototype rather than a review.

Relation to Agrio: Agrio extends the same communication principle with structured persistence, backend orchestration, Digital Twin state, ML inference, recommendations, scheduling, and command feedback.

\subsection{Balaceanu et al., 2019}

Focus: Libelium-based precision agriculture monitoring.

Main contribution: The study uses Libelium equipment to collect environmental and soil-related measurements for precision agriculture monitoring.

Use in this thesis: This paper supports the sensing and monitoring layer because it documents a concrete agricultural IoT monitoring setup.

Relation to Agrio: Agrio uses comparable sensing logic as input to a larger Digital Twin irrigation workflow.

\subsection{Bellvert et al., 2023}

Focus: Sentinel-2 assimilation into a vineyard irrigation Digital Twin.

Main contribution: The work assimilates Sentinel-2 biophysical variables into a Digital Twin to support automated vineyard irrigation scheduling.

Use in this thesis: This paper provides a field-oriented reference for Digital Twin irrigation scheduling and supports the future direction of satellite data assimilation.

Relation to Agrio: Agrio currently relies on local sensor and weather data. Satellite or UAV assimilation remains a future improvement.

\subsection{Millan et al., 2025}

Focus: Digital Twin water management in a commercial processing tomato farm.

Main contribution: The study evaluates a Digital Twin-based irrigation decision-support approach in a commercial tomato plot using soil moisture, climate data, simulation, and irrigation scheduling.

Use in this thesis: This paper is a strong reference for mature field validation because it evaluates the Digital Twin approach under commercial conditions.

Relation to Agrio: Agrio is an end-to-end engineering prototype, while this study provides stronger agronomic validation.

\subsection{Tancredi et al., 2025}

Focus: Machine-learning-enhanced Digital Twin for irrigation management.

Main contribution: The work uses sensor data and machine learning to classify irrigation decisions and reports evaluation metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score, and confusion matrix.

Use in this thesis: This paper supports the ML decision-support layer and the need to report model behavior with evaluation artifacts.

Relation to Agrio: Agrio implements Random Forest inference and exposes prediction outputs in the Digital Twin workflow, but field validation remains limited.

\subsection{El Hachimi et al., 2025}

Focus: Digital Twins and deep reinforcement learning for collective irrigation-water distribution.

Main contribution: The paper combines Digital Twins and reinforcement learning to optimize collective irrigation distribution.

Use in this thesis: This paper supports the advanced optimization discussion and future work direction.

Relation to Agrio: Agrio currently uses Random Forest prediction and supervised control rather than reinforcement learning optimization.

\subsection{Kallenberg et al., 2025}

Focus: Interoperable agricultural Digital Twins with reinforcement-learning intelligence.

Main contribution: The paper connects interoperability concepts and reinforcement-learning intelligence for agricultural Digital Twin systems.

Use in this thesis: This paper supports the state-of-the-art discussion on interoperable and intelligent Digital Twin architectures.

Relation to Agrio: Agrio is narrower and locally implemented, but its modular design can evolve toward stronger interoperability.

\subsection{Liu et al., 2025}

Focus: Cotton canopy Digital Twin under water-stress conditions.

Main contribution: The work focuses on crop-specific Digital Twin modeling under water stress.

Use in this thesis: This paper supports the discussion of plant-state modeling and crop-specific future work.

Relation to Agrio: Agrio currently models zone state and irrigation decision variables rather than detailed crop physiology.

\subsection{Ed-Daoudi et al., 2024}

Focus: Predictive irrigation with IoT and machine learning.

Main contribution: The study uses IoT data and machine-learning logic to support irrigation decisions.

Use in this thesis: This paper supports the predictive-irrigation discussion as an original applied contribution.

Relation to Agrio: Agrio integrates predictive decisions into a broader backend, dashboard, simulation, and control workflow.

\subsection{Gupta et al., 2025}

Focus: Automated IoT irrigation for water and energy efficiency.

Main contribution: The paper presents an IoT-based automated irrigation approach that connects sensing, decision logic, and actuation.

Use in this thesis: This paper supports the control and automation discussion with an original system contribution.

Relation to Agrio: Agrio adds Digital Twin state, ML inference, PostgreSQL traceability, and human-supervised validation around the actuation workflow.

\subsection{Additional original case studies}

Other original research works, such as Manocha et al. on IoT-Digital Twin smart irrigation and Qin et al. on irrigation-drainage water-salt management, may be used as supplementary comparison points when they provide concrete implemented systems or experiments. They should not replace the priority papers listed above.
