\chapter{Digital Twins in Agriculture and Irrigation: State of the Art}

\section{3.1 Review methodology}

The state-of-the-art study was built from peer-reviewed papers and technical sources related to agricultural Digital Twins, smart irrigation, IoT-based monitoring, machine learning for irrigation, and Digital Twin water management. The reviewed papers include general reviews, systematic reviews, bibliometric reviews, and application papers. The objective was not only to summarize publications, but to identify patterns, limitations, and opportunities relevant to the proposed Agrio platform.

The review was organized around five questions: (1) How are Digital Twins defined and classified in agriculture? (2) Which enabling technologies are used? (3) How are Digital Twins applied to irrigation and water management? (4) Which machine learning approaches support irrigation decisions? (5) What gaps remain in current systems, and how does Agrio address some of them?

\section{3.2 General agricultural Digital Twin reviews}

Subeesh and Chauhan [1] provide a broad review of Agricultural Digital Twins. They describe ADTs as virtual representations that support decision-making, monitoring, simulation, and optimization in smart farming. Their review emphasizes enabling technologies such as IoT, AI, AR/XR, edge computing, federated learning, and sensors. A key point is that agriculture is still at an early stage of Digital Twin adoption, and that practical implementation is complicated by the stochastic nature of weather, soil, and biological processes.

Zhang et al. [2] present a comprehensive review of Digital Twin technology in agriculture. They discuss the distinction between Digital Model, Digital Shadow, and Digital Twin, and describe architecture layers such as perception, network, and application layers. This classification is particularly useful for the present thesis because it helps evaluate whether an agricultural system is only a monitoring platform or whether it includes feedback and decision capacity.

Gund et al. [3] use bibliometric analysis to map research trends in smart agricultural Digital Twins. They show that publications have grown rapidly since 2018 and that the main clusters include precision farming, IoT integration, AI, cyber-physical systems, controlled-environment agriculture, sustainability, and food systems. This confirms that Agrio is positioned inside an active and expanding research field.

Awais et al. [4] focus on precision agriculture through Digital Twins and smart farming technologies. They note that current research often remains isolated around specific applications such as irrigation, disease detection, or crop monitoring rather than providing unified, scalable frameworks. This observation supports the need for integrated platforms like Agrio that connect multiple components: sensing, backend, database, ML, simulation, dashboard, and actuation.

\section{3.3 AI-enabled Digital Twins in agriculture}

Tsaousidis et al. [5] provide a recent review focused on AI-enabled Digital Twins in agriculture. They emphasize that many works use the term Digital Twin loosely and sometimes describe systems that are closer to decision support tools than fully synchronized Digital Twins. This warning is important for the scientific rigor of this thesis. The proposed system must therefore explicitly demonstrate its Digital Twin properties: synchronization from sensor data, virtual state, prediction, simulation, recommendation, and feedback/control capability.

The same review highlights data integration as a major bottleneck, especially when combining satellite data, local sensor data, and simulation outputs. Agrio currently integrates local sensor data, weather/forecast data, and ML outputs, but it does not yet integrate satellite or UAV data. This limitation becomes a clear future direction.

\section{3.4 Digital Twins for agricultural water management}

Ahsen et al. [6] specifically review Digital Twins for sustainable agricultural water management. Their review identifies monitoring Digital Twins and predictive Digital Twins. Monitoring Digital Twins focus on real-time response and environmental control, while predictive Digital Twins support proactive management through forecasting and simulation. Agrio combines both aspects: it monitors current zone state and also simulates future moisture evolution.

Digital Twin water management research often focuses on irrigation, hydroponics, aquaponics, and vertical farming. The main benefits include precise resource allocation, scenario planning, predictive maintenance, and optimized water usage. The main barriers include scalability, cost, data integration, and the difficulty of field deployment. These findings align with the scope and limitations of Agrio.

\section{3.5 IoT-based smart irrigation reviews}

García et al. [7] review IoT-based smart irrigation systems and analyze sensors, actuators, nodes, wireless technologies, and monitored parameters. They show that smart irrigation systems rely heavily on soil and weather monitoring. Soil moisture is a central variable, but weather parameters such as temperature, humidity, rainfall, wind, and radiation are also important. Their review supports the variable selection used in Agrio.

Obaideen et al. [8] provide an overview of smart irrigation using IoT and emphasize the relationship between irrigation systems and sustainable development. They describe smart irrigation as a system including data acquisition, irrigation control, wireless communication, data processing, and fault detection. Agrio follows these components but extends them with Digital Twin simulation and AI prediction.

Morchid et al. [10] systematically review IoT and ML applications in sustainable irrigation. Their PRISMA-based analysis highlights the benefits of real-time environmental information and ML-based decision-making, while also emphasizing barriers such as complexity, technical constraints, and limited adoption. Their work supports the decision to combine IoT, ML, and human validation rather than relying on fully autonomous decisions.

\section{3.6 Machine learning for smart irrigation}

Del-Coco et al. [9] focus on machine learning for smart irrigation. They define a generic smart irrigation system as a combination of sensors, IoT technologies, algorithms, datasets, and decision services. They discuss existing ML strategies and note open issues such as long-term data processing and the lack of annotated datasets. This is important for Agrio because the Random Forest models must be presented as part of a prototype and not as universally validated agronomic models.

The reviewed ML literature includes decision trees, Random Forests, SVM, gradient boosting, neural networks, and LSTM models. Random Forests are used in several smart irrigation studies for binary irrigation decisions or pump activation/deactivation. This supports the selection of Random Forest in Agrio, especially because the project uses structured tabular features derived from sensor and environmental data.

One challenge in ML irrigation is interpretability. Farmers may not trust a black-box output if it simply says irrigate or do not irrigate. Agrio addresses this partially by showing confidence, recommended action, suggested time, duration, and explanation in the dashboard. The Digital Twin simulation also provides visual and numerical evidence of expected moisture evolution.

\section{3.7 Directly related Digital Twin irrigation studies}

Manocha et al. [15]: IoT-Digital Twin-inspired smart irrigation for optimal water utilization. Uses IoT sensors, virtual counterparts, irrigation requirement calculation, and simulation. Very close to Agrio conceptually; useful for comparing Digital Twin maturity and architecture.

Tancredi et al. [16]: Digital Twin enhanced with ML algorithms for irrigation management using sensor data. Uses ML and sensor data for irrigation ON/OFF decisions, with evaluation metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, F1, and confusion matrix. Very close to Agrio ML layer.

Qin et al. [17]: Digital Twin-enabled intelligent irrigation-drainage system for water-salt management. Advanced system for precision water-salt management, useful as an example of deeper agronomic modeling and field-oriented Digital Twin irrigation.

Millán et al. [13]: Digital Twin for water-efficient management in a processing tomato commercial farm. Important real-field validation on a 15 ha tomato farm using soil moisture, climate data, simulation models, and daily scheduling. Useful to show what Agrio should target in future field deployment.

Bellvert et al. [14]: Sentinel-2 variables assimilated into a Digital Twin for vineyard irrigation scheduling. Demonstrates remote sensing integration, automated scheduling, and Digital Twin water balance simulation. Useful for future extension with satellite/UAV data.

El Hachimi et al. [19]: Digital Twins and deep reinforcement learning for irrigation water distribution. Advanced optimization direction beyond Agrio, useful for future work.

Liu et al. [18]: Cotton canopy Digital Twin under water stress. Shows plant-water stress modeling and crop visualization aspects, useful for discussing advanced crop-specific twins.

\section{3.8 Research gaps identified in the literature}

The reviewed literature reveals several gaps. First, many agricultural Digital Twin works remain conceptual or focused on monitoring rather than full decision and actuation loops. Second, many smart irrigation systems use threshold rules or simple dashboards without simulation. Third, ML irrigation papers often focus on model accuracy but do not integrate the model into a complete deployed system. Fourth, systems with strong agronomic validation may not provide full software architecture details. Fifth, very few academic prototypes clearly combine physical sensors, MQTT ingestion, database persistence, ML inference, Digital Twin simulation, human validation, web/mobile interfaces, scheduling, and actuator feedback in one coherent workflow.

Agrio addresses part of this gap by implementing an integrated platform. It does not claim to solve every agronomic challenge, but it demonstrates how a Digital Twin irrigation system can be architected and deployed from the physical prototype to the dashboard and pump. Its novelty is therefore mainly architectural and integrative: combining multiple layers that are often studied separately.

\section{3.9 Conclusion}

This chapter reviewed the state of the art of Digital Twins in agriculture, IoT-based smart irrigation, ML irrigation decision-making, and direct Digital Twin irrigation applications. The literature shows strong interest in DTs but also highlights fragmentation, limited field validation, and integration challenges. The next chapter compares existing works more systematically and positions Agrio with respect to scientific and engineering criteria.
